U.S. state population explorer

One CSV in, a full census briefing out. Every view moves together through 235 years of state growth, and the page rebuilds itself from any file in the same format.

JavaScriptCensus dataNYU Schack
Open the live demo

The assignment

A Schack class assignment asked us to automate U.S. state population data from 1790 to 2025 "in an interesting and efficient way." Most answers to that are a cleaned spreadsheet and a chart. I wanted the automation to be the product.

What I built

A single page that takes one CSV and rebuilds a full census briefing from it.

  • A four-step pipeline on the page (ingest, validate, compute, render) that reports what it read and flags bad rows. The sample file is 1,104 rows covering 51 areas across 25 counts.
  • A play button that moves every view through 235 years together
  • Top 12 states ranked by population, a tile map of the country, and a size-versus-growth bubble chart
  • Milestones found automatically by scanning the data for new leaders, records and firsts
  • A profile for any state, with a trend projection to 2040 and a comparison against a second state
  • Regional share of U.S. population by Census Bureau region
  • A growth leaderboard between any two counts, sortable, with CAGR and rank change, and a copy-as-CSV button

Load a different file in the same format and the whole page rebuilds.

Notes on the data

Source is the U.S. Census Bureau decennial counts from 1790 to 2020. Years that don't end in 0 are treated as estimates. Early counts include territories before statehood. Projections extend each state's recent growth rate and are not demographic forecasts.
